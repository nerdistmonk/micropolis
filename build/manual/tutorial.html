Tutorial -- A Walk Through Your City

Starting Up

To start Micropolis, run the program named "micropolis_sdl3" from the game directory. Micropolis loads its graphics, sounds, and other data from the directory it is started in, so start it from there. A splash screen appears for a few seconds. Press any key or click the mouse to dismiss it.

The game opens with a generated city already in place, so you can start building right away. To start a new city or play a historical scenario, use the File & Scenario Manager, described below.

Getting Help

If you want to know what a control or graphic does, press F1 to turn on help mode, then click the control. A help window explains it. Press F1 or Escape to leave help mode. The User Reference section explains each control in more detail.

Generating a New City

Click "File" in the top bar to open the File & Scenario Manager, then open the "Save / Load" tab. Choose a difficulty of Easy, Medium, or Hard. You can also set the terrain options. Then click "New City Map". A dialog asks whether to keep your current city or start another one. Choose "Another city!" to generate the new map.

Easy is a good choice when you're starting out.

Starting a Scenario

Open the File & Scenario Manager and select the "Scenarios" tab. Click a historical scenario, then confirm on the dialog that appears. When the scenario ends, a dialog reports whether you met the city charter objectives.

City Name

Open City Hall by clicking the city name and population display in the top bar, then select the "City Info" tab. Click the "City Name" field and type a new name. Click anywhere else to finish editing.

Main Screen

Your city is now ready for play. The main screen has a status bar across the top, a toolbar down the left edge, and the map in the middle. The top bar shows the city name and population, and holds buttons for Funds, City, Overlays, Disasters, File, and Speed. A message ticker appears just below the top bar when the city has news. Click the ticker to dismiss it.

Map and Overlays

The Radar Map in the lower left corner shows your whole city. Click it to move the main view to that spot. The Overlays button in the top bar chooses which view the map shows. The choices include Power Grid, Traffic Density, Pollution Map, Crime Rate, and Land Value, among others.

The Query Tool button sits above the Demand gauge. Select it, then click a tile on the map to open an inspector with information about that spot.

The Demand gauge shows the demand for Residential, Commercial, and Industrial zones, and can be helpful in planning your city.

Mouse and Keyboard

Click the left mouse button on the map to use the selected tool, or drag to use it along a line. Hold Control while dragging to keep the motion in a straight line. Drag the map with the right mouse button to scroll the view. Shift and right click bulldozes a tile.

Press the Space bar to pause or resume the simulation. Keys 1, 2, and 3 set the speed to Slow, Medium, and Fast. You can also click the Speed button in the top bar to cycle through the speeds.

Use the arrow keys, or A, E, comma, and O, to scroll the map. Press X and Z to select the next or previous tool. Hold B, R, P, or T to use the Bulldozer, Roadway, Power Line, or Railroad. Release the key to return to the tool you were using.

Building a City

To begin a city, we need: places for Sims to live, places for Sims to work, and power.

The toolbar on the left edge has the tools for building: zones (Residential, Commercial, and Industrial), services (Fire Station and Police Department), utilities (Power Lines, Coal Power Plant, and Nuclear Power Plant), transportation (Roadway and Railroad), the Bulldozer, and buildings (Sports Stadium, Seaport, Municipal Airport, and Park). Hover over a tool to see its name and cost.

You can only build on Clear Land, so use the Bulldozer to clear away some trees. Select the Bulldozer, then drag it across the trees to clear a large area of land to prepare for building.

Select the Residential tool and click or drag on the Clear Land to place Residential Zones, where Sims will live. Place a few zones next to each other.

Now decide where to place a power plant. Select the Coal Power Plant and place it in some open space near your Residential Zones. If the power plant is not directly adjacent to a zone, you'll need to lay Power Lines from the plant to the zones. Select the Power Lines tool and drag it from the plant toward your zones. Adjacent Power Line sections connect to each other automatically. Roads and Railroads connect in the same way.

Zones that are adjacent to a powered zone do not need their own Power Lines. Once the zones are powered, small houses start to appear as the Sims move in.

Once there are a few Residential Zones, where Sims can live, you need to make it possible for your new residents to find jobs. They can't all work at the power plant! Place some Commercial and Industrial Zones near your Residential ones, and connect them with Power Lines.

Next, use the Roadway tool to connect your Residential housing to the Commercial and Industrial areas, so the Sims can commute to work. Road sections connect themselves like Power Line sections. Once you have roads, traffic will be generated.

Budget and Taxes

Click the Funds button in the top bar to open City Hall on the Budget tab. Here you can set the funding levels for your city services. The Taxes tab sets the tax rate. When you are finished, click "Apply & Close".

If "Auto-Budget Processing" is turned off in the File & Scenario Manager's Options tab, City Hall opens on the Budget tab at each budget cycle so you can review your budget. If it is turned on, the budget is applied automatically.

Graphs and Evaluation

Unlike the map, which shows the current state of your city, the graphs give you a record of the past, so you can gauge trends and cycles. Open the Graphs tab in City Hall, and switch between the "10 Years" and "120 Years" views.

The Evaluation tab in City Hall shows how the city is doing overall.

Saving and Loading

Click "File" in the top bar and select the "Save / Load" tab. Click "Save City" to open the system save dialog. Cities are saved with the ".cty" extension. To load a city, click "Load City" and choose the file in the system open dialog.

For quick saves, press F7 to save to quicksave.cty and F8 to load it. Quick saves are written to the directory the game was started from.

This is all the basic information you need to run Micropolis, but we suggest reading on. The User Reference section explains in detail how to use each program function. Inside Micropolis explains the inner workings of the simulator, and gives some brief hints and tips for using it. There is also an essay on The History of Cities and City Planning, and a Bibliography for serious City Planners.
